# Hockey V3 physics and actual limitations

`logic/hockey-physics.js` is an original dependency-free pure model shared by the browser adapter and Node tests. The browser artwork remains cached vector artwork from V2. Physics runs at a fixed 240Hz with a capped frame accumulator. A rendered frame can consume multiple physics steps, but pause supplies no elapsed time.

Paddles have bounded target areas (x74–276, player y285–396, rival y74–185), maximum speed, acceleration and braking; touching another point updates a target instead of teleporting a paddle. Contacts use puck velocity relative to the moving paddle, an inward normal impulse, penetration projection, and an explicit maximum puck speed. Alternating wall/contact projection prevents a correction from leaving the puck outside a wall. Rail reflection only reverses inward velocity. Small steps and goal-post circles handle the goal throat; scoring requires the puck to cross the full-puck goal line inside the mouth. Every goal resets with a visible countdown, and a winning goal ends the match exactly once.

Easy/medium/hard use different reaction delays (.24/.12/.06s), speed limits (130/185/245 logical units per second), acceleration limits (850/1250/1750), and prediction errors (29/12/3). Prediction reflects an intercept through sidewall bounces. The AI uses the same physical paddle movement and collision rules. Near an upper corner it performs a finite-speed retreat so it does not camp over and pin a puck; the paddle boundary inset leaves a full-puck escape corridor at each side rail. Slow/normal/fast scale initial serve speed and movement limits by .8/1/1.25. Target scores are 3,5,7 or10.

The model tracks player shots, paddle hits, saves, elapsed active time, rival hits, and recovery count. A save means an incoming puck contacted the player paddle near the defensive end. A shot means a player contact sent the puck toward the rival. The result screen reports those counters. A won difficulty unlocks its corresponding completion tier. V3 points are stored through the root's `recordScore`; original records remain untouched.

A stall monitor measures integrated travel, occupied-position span near contacts, and speed; it can detect contact jitter with high reported velocity but low displacement. It first applies a bounded centerward velocity correction without relocating the puck. If two corrections fail within a short window, it performs a logged neutral serve with no points. Recoveries are visible to the player and retained in model debug/snapshots. A recoverable impossible legacy snapshot keeps match scores and gets a neutral serve; malformed snapshots cannot enable Continue. Finite restored values and scores are validated, and paddles are brought into their valid target areas.

Tests: `node tests/hockey-physics.cjs` and `node tests/hockey-legacy-regression.cjs`. They cover frame-cadence determinism; legacy-corner escape; relative moving-paddle contacts; centered overlap; bounded movement/energy; legal goals and post misses; physical correction and neutral fallback; old snapshot normalization; distinct AI constraints; every target setting; malformed data; all four rail corners, simultaneous side/contact cases, maximum-speed no-tunnel contact, saved exact goal-line crossings, bounded recovery-log restoration, serve-to-conceding-side behavior, and five simulated minutes of seeded extreme-input endurance. The current suite has25 physics regressions, plus the preserved legacy fixture.

This is a 2D disc simulation with kinematic finite-speed paddles, not a full rigid-body or aerodynamic solver. There is no puck spin or paddle mass simulation. Tests establish the stated invariants for deterministic scenarios and seeded endurance, not mathematical proof of every possible collision. Physical iPhone testing remains separate from headless/browser testing. Main-thread overload beyond the accumulator cap deliberately slows simulation instead of advancing physics by a giant unstable step.

Final independent review added270 valid corner/profile cases (648,000 fixed steps), with no permanent trap or recovery events. Invalid/inherited option names now normalize through strict enumerations without object coercion; that defensive API regression is included in the25 tests.
